# Bible entry templates

Skeletons for [`writing-bible`](../SKILL.md). Keep only the fields a project
uses; delete the rest rather than leaving empty fields, because an empty field
invites invention at exactly the moment discipline matters. Every entry ends
with its status, first appearance, and one-line story job.

Statuses: `established` (binding — prose may not contradict it), `planned`
(intended, not yet on the page — safe to change), `disputed` (conflicting
established facts; carry a note naming both sources).

## Person

```markdown
## <Name> [status] — job: <what story work this character does>

- Want: <what they visibly pursue>
- Need: <what would actually fix their life> (distance from want = arc)
- Lie: <the false belief the story presses on>
- Voice: <speech tells — length, register, favorite words, what they never say>
- Relationships: <who, and the tension in one line each>
- Arc: positive | flat | negative — <shape in one line>
- History: <appearing chapter → what changed for them, what they learned>
```

## Place

```markdown
## <Place> [status]

- Feels like: <light, weather, mood — what it does to the people in it>
- Who runs it / who it costs
- Cost of entering or leaving (social, literal)
- Texture: <the three concrete details scenes here should reach for>
```

## System (magic, technology, government, sport)

```markdown
## <System> [status]

- Does: <what it enables>
- Cannot: <hard limits> ← limits are the story engine; fill first
- Costs: <what using it takes — resources, time, self, relationships>
- Who knows: <who knows it exists; who knows the limits>
- Established uses: <chapter — what was shown, so prose can't accidentally
  exceed it>
```

## Culture / faction

```markdown
## <Culture / faction>

- Values: <what they admire, what they punish>
- Taboos: <what no member does casually>
- Daily texture: <food, money, work, weather — where prose texture comes from>
- Idiom/naming: <what they call things the reader knows by other names>
```

## Event

```markdown
## <Event> [status]

- When: <in-world date or era anchor>
- What happened: <two lines max>
- Who remembers / who was there
- Set in motion: <what it started that is still running>
```

## Ledgers

```markdown
## Open questions

| Question | Raised (ch) | Payoff due (chapter/book) |

## Who-knows-what

| Fact | <Character A> | <Character B> | Reader | (ch learned per holder) |

## Retcon log

| Date | What changed | Old fact appeared in | Fixed? |

## Timeline

| When | Event | Source (ch) |
```

## Style sheet

Naming conventions (phonetic rules per culture), units, calendar, forms of
address, banned anachronisms. Small, and load-bearing: this is the page that
keeps book four consistent with book one when memory alone can't.
