# Fiction rubric

Passes for [`writing-critique`](../SKILL.md), in order. Each pattern has a
**test** to run and the **reader cost** it imposes. Severity tracks reader cost,
not fix difficulty. Fiction lives or dies on momentum, interiority, and trust —
most findings reduce to one of those three being broken.

## Pass 1 — Engine (spine)

- **No want on the table.** Test: by the end of the first act (or first scene,
  in short work), can you say what the protagonist is pursuing and what happens
  if they fail? **Cost:** the reader has nothing to root for; tension never
  starts. _Blocking._
- **Dramatic question unanswerable.** Test: name the "will she…?" question. If
  nothing fits, the story has no promise to keep. **Cost:** suspense never
  starts; the reader is watching events, not a story.
- **Flat stakes.** Test: name what the protagonist loses at each failure. If the
  answer is always "nothing yet", stakes never exist. **Cost:** no cost, no
  caring. _Blocking_ if nothing ever costs anything.
- **Want/need confusion.** Test: is there a gap between what the protagonist
  pursues and what they need to learn? If not, the ending will be a prize, not
  an arc — acceptable for flat-arc stories, otherwise the engine is missing.
- **Unearned ending.** Test: does the climax resolve through what the
  protagonist _did_ (choice, cost, earned skill) or through luck, a rescuer, or
  an unexplained reversal? Coincidence may cause problems; it may never solve
  them. **Cost:** the promise breaks exactly at payoff. _Blocking._

## Pass 2 — Structure

- **And-then scenes.** Test: connect each scene to the next with _therefore_ or
  _but_. Any pair joined only by _and then_ is a dead scene or half a scene.
  **Cost:** chronology instead of story; momentum dies quietly.
- **No-turn scene.** Test: for each scene, name what is different at the end —
  door closed, belief cracked, fact learned. No turn means summary wearing a
  scene's clothes. **Cost:** the reader can skip it and lose nothing, and
  eventually does.
- **Lopsided try-fail.** Test: count attempts before the climax. One try that
  works reads as luck; a success should cost (yes-but) and a failure should
  worsen (no-and). **Cost:** tension has no floor to stand on.
- **Escalation stall.** Test: does each reversal outrank the last by _personal
  cost_, or just by volume? **Cost:** noise replaces tension; the reader
  numbs.
- **Sequel-starved.** Test: after a blow, does the character react, weigh a
  dilemma, and decide? Reactionless prose reads as event-log; the reader never
  gets to feel the blow land. **Cost:** no one cares about the next scene,
  because the character demonstrably didn't care about the last one.
- **Stalled middle.** Test: name what changed about the pursuit in each act-2
  block. Blocks with no progress on want or need are treading water.
  **Cost:** the long, famous sagginess.

## Pass 3 — Scene and POV

- **Head-hopping.** Test: pick the scene's POV character; any paragraph inside
  another head mid-scene is drift (deliberate omniscient aside, agreed and
  consistent, is different — this test catches accident). **Cost:** the reader
  falls out of the scene and back into being an audience.
- **Distant camera on a hot moment.** Test: in the scene's key moment, is the
  reader inside the POV character's senses, or watching from across the room?
  Narrative distance should be closest where the cost is highest. **Cost:** the
  scene's peak lands as reportage.
- **Summarized what should be rendered.** Test: is a turning beat told
  ("they argued for hours") where the scene needed to happen in real time?
  **Cost:** the reader watches the story skip its own important parts.
- **Scene entry/exit.** Test: does the scene open as late as it can (cut the
  arrival, the small talk, the waking up) and leave on the turn rather than
  after it? **Cost:** dead minutes at both seams; pace drags everywhere at once.

## Pass 4 — Line (fiction)

- **Filters.** _She saw/felt/noticed/realized X_ puts the character's attention
  between the reader and the thing. **Cost:** we're told about the moment
  instead of being in it. (Deep-POV passages especially.)
- **Emotion at secondhand.** _She felt devastated._ **Cost:** the reader is
  handed a label and does no feeling.
- **Puppet dialogue.** Test: could two characters' lines be swapped without
  loss? Does anyone say exactly what they mean with no cost? **Cost:** people
  become mouthpieces; the scene's tension is stated, not played.
- **As-you-know-Bob.** Test: is anyone told something both speakers already
  know for the reader's benefit? **Cost:** the world feels like a set.
- **Melodrama.** Reaction outruns stimulus: weeping at a hangnail, thunder at a
  stubbed toe. **Cost:** the reader's trust in the story's scale.
- **Filterless exposition dump.** A paragraph of world-fact with no one living
  it. **Cost:** the story stops.
- **Monotone prose.** Uniform sentence lengths, uniform openings, symmetric
  constructions on every line. **Cost:** machine music; the reader stops
  hearing the narrator as a person.

## Pass 5 — Embodiment and texture

- **Generic texture.** Test: could this setting description belong to any
  hallway in any building? Replace with what only this place has — the detail
  that costs the writer something to know. **Cost:** the story floats free of
  ground; the reader skims description by habit.
- **Weather nobody needs.** Weather earns its place when it costs someone
  something or does work in the scene.
- **Interiority vacuum.** Test: pages of event with no thought, or pages of
  thought with no event — the balance is the art; the vacuum on either side is
  the finding. **Cost:** neither head nor heartbeat.
- **Coincidence direction.** Luck that makes things worse is story; luck that
  saves the day is cheating (Pass 1, unearned ending — checked again here at
  scene grain).

## Severity anchors

- _Blocking_: no engine, unearned climax, POV broken at a load-bearing scene,
  stakes that never land.
- _Major_: no-turn scenes, puppet dialogue, distant camera on the hot moment,
  stalled middle.
- _Minor_: tag adverbs, generic texture, rhythm uniformity — real, but the
  piece survives them.
