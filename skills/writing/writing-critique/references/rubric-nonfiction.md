# Nonfiction rubric

Passes for [`writing-critique`](../SKILL.md), in order. Each pattern carries a
**test** and a **reader cost**. Severity is judged by reader cost, never by how
easy the fix is.

## Pass 1 — Spine

- **No spine.** Test: state the piece's claim in one sentence without "and". If
  it takes two, or you can only name the topic, there is no spine. **Cost:** the
  reader finishes without knowing what they were told; every block competes
  with every other. _Blocking._
- **Buried spine.** Test: is the claim made in the opening, in the reader's
  terms? A spine that arrives after setup makes the opening disposable. **Cost:**
  the reader commits attention without knowing to what.
- **Spine drift.** Test: name the spine each block serves. A block serving a
  second spine is drift — re-thread or split the piece. **Cost:** the piece
  promises one thing, delivers another; the reader re-orients and resents it.
- **Topic, not claim.** Test: could the opposite also be argued from the same
  material? Then the spine is a subject, not a position. **Cost:** information
  with no point — a report, not a piece.

## Pass 2 — Structure

- **Discovery order.** Test: does the order match how the writer found the
  material (context, false start, the real point) rather than what the reader
  needs first? **Cost:** the reader does the writer's thinking, and quits.
- **Buried lede.** Test: is the payoff the first thing the reader can act on, or
  does it wait behind warming up? **Cost:** skimming; the reader leaves before
  the point.
- **Orphan block.** Test: for each block, state the job it does that the block
  before it did not. No job, no block. **Cost:** length without progress.
- **Missing prerequisite.** Test: list what each block leans on; anything the
  stated reader doesn't hold and no earlier block grounded is a gap. **Cost:**
  silent reader dropout.
- **Sequence instead of logic.** Test: does each block connect by _therefore /
  but_, or merely by _also / next_? **Cost:** the piece reads as a list; the
  reader supplies the argument the writer withheld.
- **No ending.** Test: does the last block land the spine, or restate and stop?
  A summary that adds nothing is not an ending. **Cost:** the payoff promised
  never arrives.

## Pass 3 — Paragraph

- **Two jobs in one paragraph.** Test: state the paragraph's topic in one
  sentence; if it takes "and", split. **Cost:** the point dissolves in the blend.
- **No-topic paragraph.** Test: name what the paragraph is about without
  paraphrasing its first sentence. **Cost:** the reader reads forward, waiting
  for a point that never comes.
- **Bloated middle.** Test: is the paragraph's middle cushioning between two
  good sentences? **Cost:** the reader skims and misses the point.
- **Format mismatch.** Parallel items → list; argument → prose; same shape
  repeating 3+ times → table. **Cost:** prose buries scannable structure; lists
  flatten arguments into bullets that no longer connect.

## Pass 4 — Sentence

Throat-clearing (delete the first clause — anything lost?); nominalization
(find the actor and the action — "the implementation was completed" hides
both); hidden verbs (`is indicative of` → `indicates`); hedges that protect the
writer rather than inform the reader; dangling referents (for every _this_,
_it_, _that_, _they_, name the noun); two-job sentences (unrelated claims
joined by a comma); monotone rhythm (uniform openings or lengths flatten
emphasis). Full patterns with examples: the line-edit reference in
`writing-edit`. **Cost of the whole pass:** the prose reads as output, and the
reader stops trusting the narrator's hand.

## Pass 5 — Evidence

- **Claim without support.** Find the sentence carrying each assertion. **Cost:**
  the reader either believes blindly or believes nothing.
- **Evidence without claim.** Name the claim each number or example serves; an
  orphan fact is a _so what?_ the writer left undrawn.
- **Unearned superlative.** _Best, first, leading, fastest_ — name the
  comparison and the source. **Cost:** publication risk. Route to
  `writing-verify`.
- **Causal leap.** _Causes, drives, leads to_ with correlation but no
  mechanism. **Cost:** the skeptical reader discounts the whole piece on one
  leap.
- **Missing counterargument.** For a persuasive piece, state the strongest
  opposing case and check it's addressed. **Cost:** the piece preaches to the
  converted and knows it.
- **Quoted where paraphrase would do** — or paraphrased where the wording was
  the point. **Cost:** buried points, lost arguments.
